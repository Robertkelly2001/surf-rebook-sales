Assigned case: F — Atlantic Edge Surf School

Behind the build

Task 1 planning, assumptions and Task 2 testing for the Surf Rebook assessment prototype.

Hunch — original wording

Small Irish surf schools using paper diaries and WhatsApp to manage any booking and im finding it very hard to see how this business became anything because they don't have an online presence just kind of like a word of mouth thing where if you know about it you can attend it kind of thing. I think the owner should create a website or make an app where users who came before can just rebook and he can make a little pfofit off the app by selling it himself as a more easier way to get back in to the surf school.

This original wording is preserved unchanged. It was supplied during an AI-assisted discussion; it is not represented here as verified pre-AI work.

Later pricing thought — revised wording

I don't know how much an app costs to create but we will say 100 euro to start up and we say about 30 quid to keep it running monthly surely he will pay staff more than 30 quid if 2 clients cancel that's 2 hours if staff were paid 15 quid an hour this app would be a no brainer after 2 clients cancel.

Assumption check: €100 setup and €30 per month are proposed customer prices, not verified app development or running costs. Niamh is the owner in the case. An illustrative value of €15 per hour means two hours of staff time saved per month would equal the €30 monthly fee, excluding setup. The case does not say that two cancellations take two hours, and actual savings have not been measured.

Later clarification: the case does not establish whether the school has a website or relies on word of mouth. The agreed alpha is an internal cancellation and rebooking app. Customers do not book themselves in this version.

Niche, buyer and user

Niche: small Irish surf schools using paper diaries and WhatsApp to manage bookings, where weather cancellations create manual rebooking work.

Seller: the consultancy developing a generic version of the agreed solution, with the case owner's permission.

Buyer: the surf-school owner who decides whether to adopt and pay for the system.

Daily user: the owner or staff member managing cancelled lessons and contacting customers.

Invented customer pain statement: “Every cancelled day costs me hours of admin, and some customers never come back.” This is illustrative wording, not a direct quotation or real testimonial.

Technology and the current way of working

An internal booking and rebooking management app. In the case, Niamh rebooks customers by phone and WhatsApp from a paper diary. The alpha brings cancelled bookings into one list, offers sessions with enough places and shows the lesson value still awaiting rebooking.

Staff still decide whether conditions are safe and contact customers separately. The alpha does not predict the weather or automatically chase customers.

Two existing services with overlapping features

These are the booking examples considered in the initial notes. They are comparisons from other sectors, not direct surf-school competitors.

1. OpenTable

Its reservation management service lets restaurants accept, view and manage online reservations in one place. The overlap is organising bookings and availability. Surf Rebook instead demonstrates moving groups after a surf session is cancelled. No universal one-hour change policy is assumed.

Business model — assumption

€100 one-off setup plus €30 per month for a proposed future supported version. This is a pricing assumption, not validated market research or a currently available subscription.

The reasoning is that reducing manual rebooking administration could free staff time for running lessons, looking after the school and helping customers. Attracting more customers is a possible longer-term benefit, not a demonstrated result of this alpha. The actual time saved and willingness to pay would need to be tested. The demo remains free and does not take payments.

Lean product plan — five lines

  1. Hypothesis: surf-school owners will request a demo of an app that helps rearrange cancelled lessons and track money at risk.
  2. Audience: small Irish surf schools using paper diaries and WhatsApp.
  3. Call to action: request a free demo before any proposed paid adoption.
  4. Success: a proposed target of at least five demo requests from 50 relevant visitors within four weeks. This is a future test target, not an achieved result. This assessment form sends no data and the site has no analytics, so it does not measure that conversion target.
  5. Out of scope: taking payments, predicting weather and letting customers book themselves.

Alpha test

What I tried: I opened the published demo on my phone, cancelled a session, rebooked a group, checked that the Money total changed and refreshed the page to check that the changes stayed saved. All of these worked in my test.

What confused me during review: the first version showed the lesson start time but not when it ended. I also felt the design was too boxy and old-school.

What changed: I asked for start and end times, so the app now shows ranges such as 09:00–10:30. I also asked for better visuals, and the design was updated with ocean colours, wave artwork, softer layouts and floating navigation, while retaining large buttons.

Retest: the successful phone test was reported after these updates. No additional failure was reported during that phone test.

Separately, AI-assisted automated logic checks covered capacity, cancellation, rebooking, overdue boundaries, calculations, saved-state reload, undo and reset. These checks used a simulated environment; they were not a visual browser test or a substitute for my phone test.

Build assumptions and limits

  • Lessons last 90 minutes in the demo. The case does not specify duration. Six sequential slots run from 09:00 to 18:00.
  • A booking means one group; free places and revenue use the number of people in each group. Capacity is 12 people per session and lesson value is €45 per person.
  • The sample timetable starts on the first load or reset date and covers eight days. Future sessions are offered in time order; if fewer than three suitable sessions remain, the app says so.
  • The 48-hour warning starts when the session is cancelled. Previously waiting sample bookings demonstrate the warning immediately.
  • All 21 booking records and contacts are fictional sample data. Money at risk is potential lesson revenue awaiting rebooking, not proven lost income or cash collected.
  • Browser storage persists changes in the same browser. It is not a shared database, secure operational system or cross-device service.
  • The landing-page form only displays a thank-you message. It does not send, save or collect a real demo request.
  • The landing-page visual is an illustrative sample, not a live view of the demo's stored bookings.
